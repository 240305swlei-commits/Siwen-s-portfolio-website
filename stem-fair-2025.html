STEM Fair Research Project 2025

American Stem Prep · Plant Biology & Mechatronics Automation

Project Overview

This study investigates whether the direction of gravitational pull directly affects the directional growth patterns of plant roots. By utilizing an automated Arduino rotation system, we continuously altered the orientation of experimental seeds to disrupt their gravity perception.

To establish an uncompromised baseline where environmental factors are perfectly uniform, all seeds were cultivated within an identical growth medium environment. We tightly controlled localized moisture indices, structural boundaries, and ambient temperatures by equalizing the concentration profiles of our custom agar-agar solutions.

Hypothesis

"If the direction of gravitational force directly determines the orientation of plant root development, then continuously rotating the seeds using an Arduino system will disrupt their gravity tracking and cause the roots to grow in altered, non-vertical directions compared to static control plants."

Agar Solution Formulation & Execution Plan

A rigorous seven-step laboratory preparation protocol was deployed to guarantee that the chemical composition and structural density of the growth medium remained perfectly uniform across both testing groups:

  1. Prepare exactly 1.5g of Agar powder in a graduated cylinder and measure 100mL of distilled water inside a laboratory beaker, targeted around a precise powder-to-liquid saturation balance.
  2. Combine both components together in a clean heating pot and thoroughly mix until the solution looks fully saturated and a minor amount of undissolved agar powder remains suspended.
  3. Gradually increase the localized heat settings of the pot until the combined mixture reaches a steady, active boil.
  4. Continuously stir and agitate the agar and water mixture throughout the entire heating cycle to eliminate clumping.
  5. Carefully add minute increments of water if residual undissolved agar powder remains visible, bringing the mixture to a perfectly saturated state.
  6. Once the solution reaches its boiling peak, reduce the heat source completely and allow the hot agar mixture to cool undisturbed for 5 to 8 minutes.
  7. Carefully pour the uniform liquid agar solution into the sterile petri dishes and allow them to sit for another 20 to 30 minutes until the medium cools completely and solidifies into a firm gel structure.

Conclusion & Discussion

The data compiled throughout this study strongly validates our research objective. Because the agar powder consistency, liquid proportions, moisture retention, and environmental temperatures were kept entirely identical, any alterations in root path geometry can be directly attributed to the changing directional vectors of gravity caused by the Arduino motor rig.

Hypothesis Evaluation: Confirmed
The experiment explicitly proves that the direction of gravity heavily governs plant growth patterns. Removing a fixed gravitational anchor caused roots to drop their standard downward paths, confirming that gravity acts as the core directional guide for early root infrastructure.
